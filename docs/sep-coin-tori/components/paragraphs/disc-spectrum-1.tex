The period spectrum is highly structured. The five most populated
periods are $N=54$ ($659$ sets), $N=58$ ($361$ sets), $N=30$ ($332$ sets), $N=50$ ($275$ sets), $N=24$ ($263$ sets). These values are multiples of many
small integers, so multiple 1D cycles share that period and the
resulting 2D cross-product is large. By contrast, the sparsest
periods are $N=2$ ($1$), $N=4$ ($16$), $N=28$ ($23$), $N=42$ ($25$), $N=32$ ($44$). These values admit 1D revivals on
only a small subset of cycles, and their 2D cross-products are
correspondingly thin. The polarisation between dense and sparse
periods is a direct reflection of the arithmetic of the 1D
revival sets.
