\emph{Period structure.}
Table~\ref{tab:torus-period-n60} shows the revival periods observed
on each torus, for $N \le 60$ and $N = 120$. A bullet marks each $(k, m)$ and $N$
where at least one distinct revival exists. The distribution is
highly uneven. The tori with a $k = 2$ axis contribute the smallest
families (Table~\ref{tab:tori-summary}): the $2$-cycle has only
three signatures, at minimal periods $2$, $24$, and $24$, and the
resulting torus families are correspondingly sparse. The largest
families occur on the tori $4 \times 4$, $3 \times 4$, and
$3 \times 3$, where the dense 1D spectra of the $3$- and $4$-cycles
allow many parameter combinations to share a common period. The
period $N = 60$ appears on the most tori, because the $5$- and
$10$-cycle signatures all have minimal period $60$, which dominates
the lcm with any admissible axis period; every torus with a $5$ or
$10$ axis therefore inherits that period.  The $5 \times 8$ and the
$8 \times 8$ tori have there first revival at $N = 120$.
