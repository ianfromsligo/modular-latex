\par
A notable asymmetry emerges between square and non-square tori
at the level of the reduced classification. Square tori $L \times L$
admit both symmetric realisations ($\rho_x = \rho_y$) and
asymmetric ones, and the symmetric family generally contributes
the largest block of parameter sets at any given period. On the
non-square tori, revivals are more constrained: the two cycle
lengths must share a revival period, and the reflection values on
the two axes must be tuned separately to satisfy the revival
condition. This constraint is what makes the non-square tori
structurally distinct from their square counterparts.
