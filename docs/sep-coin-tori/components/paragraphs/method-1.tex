The tori revival classification is built and verified in a single notebook,
\texttt{EXP033.ipynb}, available in the accompanying repository
\cite{full_revivals_references}.  This notebook refers to projective
and exact (Perfec) revivals.  .
A \emph{projective} revival at period $N$ means
$U^N = e^{i\phi} I$ for some global phase $\phi$; the state returns
to itself up to an unobservable phase. An \emph{exact} or perfect revival at
period $N$ means $U^N = I$; this is the $\phi = 1$ case, and is the
criterion used by Dukes and adopted here. Every exact revival is
projective, but not conversely. 

The notebook proceeds in three
stages: a direct demonstration of the two types of revivals, an
operator-level verification of the 1D signature set, and a
tensor-product enumeration of the 2D classification. The
enumeration stage includes a reduction pass that collapses harmonic
duplicates, onlu listing each distinct parameter set at its
first exact revival period. All code, data, and result files
are produced by the notebook and packaged as a single archive
\cite{full_revivals_references}.

\emph{Direct demonstration.} We evolve the walk step by step and
record the fidelity of the state against its initial value,
\begin{equation}
F(N) = |\langle \psi_0 | \psi_N \rangle|^2 ,
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
together with two operator-level diagnostics: the exact residual
$\|U^N - I\|$ and the projective residual
$\max_j |\lambda_j^N - \overline{\lambda^N}|$, where
$\lambda_j$ are the eigenvalues of $U$ and
$\overline{\lambda^N}$ is their $N$-th power mean. We demonstrate
the two criteria on $k = 2$ and $k = 4$, both at $\rho = 1/2$ and
$\delta \in \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$. In every case the fidelity is zero
at odd steps and reaches exactly $1$ at $N = 6$ and every multiple
of six; the projective residual vanishes at each such step with
phase $\phi = \pm i$ at $N = 6$, $\phi = -1$ at $N = 12$,
$\phi = \mp i$ at $N = 18$, and $\phi = 1$ at $N = 24$. Only the
$N = 24$ revival is exact. The $k = 2$ exact revival at $N = 24$
lies in Dukes' seed family but was not tabulated; the $k = 4$
exact revival at $N = 24$ is tabulated in Dukes' Table 4. The
$N = 6$ revival is projective only and belongs to neither the
exact catalogue nor a gap in Dukes. As controls we reproduce two
revivals from Dukes' tables, at $k = 4$, $\rho = 3/4$, $\delta = 0$
(period 6) and $k = 3$, $\rho = 2/3$, $\delta = 0$ (period 8), and
confirm the tabulated periods. The exact residual at these
revivals is of order $10^{-15}$ in every case.

\emph{1D revival verification.} We transcribe the complete set
of 1D revival solutions tabulated by Dukes for cycle lengths
$k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$, up to period $N = 30$ for
$k \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\}$ and $N = 60$ for $k \in \{5, 8, 10\}$,
together with the $k = 2$, $\rho = 1/2$, $\delta \in \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$
exact revivals at $N = 24$ that lie in Dukes' seed family but were
not tabulated, giving 172 quadruples $(k, \rho, \delta, N)$. We
verify each at the operator level using the projective eigenvalue
residual
\begin{equation}
r(k, \rho, \delta, N)
= \max_j \left| \lambda_j^N - \overline{\lambda^N} \right| ,
\label{eq:eig-residual}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_j$ are the eigenvalues of $U_k(\rho, \delta)$ and
$\overline{\lambda^N}$ is their $N$-th power mean. The residual
vanishes when all $\lambda_j^N$ share a common value, i.e.\ when
$U^N = e^{i\phi} I$ for some $\phi$; the exact revival condition
$U^N = I$ is the special case $\phi = 1$, and is confirmed
separately by a matrix-power check at the recorded $N$. All 172
entries pass with $r < 10^{-6}$; the worst residual in the set is
$2.3 \times 10^{-13}$, at $k = 10$, $\rho = (5 - \sqrt{5})/10$,
$\delta = 6\pi/5$, $N = 60$. We use the eigenvalue formulation
rather than the matrix-power test as the primary check because the
latter accumulates floating-point error at large $N$; the
matrix-power check is retained as a confirmation at the recorded
period.

\emph{2D enumeration.} For a separable walk on a $k \times m$
torus, the single-step operator factorises as
$U_{k \times m} = U_k \otimes U_m$. A full-state exact revival
occurs at period $N$ when $U_k^N = I$ and $U_m^N = I$, or more
generally when the product of the two factor phases is $1$; the
latter includes the case in which each factor revives only
projectively, with phases that are inverses of each other. We
enumerate all ordered pairs $(k, m)$ with
$k, m \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ and $k \le m$, and every pair
of 1D revival signatures on those cycles. For each pair we
diagonalise $U_{k \times m}$ once, scan $N$ from $1$ to $420$,
and derive the exact period from the phase order via
$N_{\text{exact}} = N_{\text{proj}} \cdot \mathrm{ord}(\phi)$,
where $\mathrm{ord}(\phi)$ is the smallest $r$ with $\phi^r = 1$.
Scanning every $N$ rather than only multiples of the factor LCM
is necessary because the phase-cancellation case can revive
exactly at a proper divisor of the LCM. Each canonical record is
then confirmed by a matrix-power check $\|U^N - I\| < 10^{-10}$ at
its exact period. The enumeration is exhaustive by construction:
every 2D revival is a tensor product of 1D revivals, so the
closure of the 1D catalogue under tensor product is the complete
2D classification.

The enumeration writes two data files. The first,
\texttt{2d\_classification.csv}, is the reduced classification: one
row per distinct parameter set
$(k, m, \rho_x, \delta_x, \rho_y, \delta_y)$ recorded at its
smallest exact revival period, containing 18{,}174 entries after
the reduction pass. The second, \texttt{2d\_classification\_full.csv},
records every exact revival period at which each parameter set
revives, containing 103{,}427 entries. The projective counterparts
\texttt{2d\_classification\_proj.csv} and
\texttt{2d\_classification\_full\_proj.csv} are included as
supplementary data for the Discussion. All four carry 1D
provenance at the factor level; the 2D provenance is the tensor
product of the two factor labels.
