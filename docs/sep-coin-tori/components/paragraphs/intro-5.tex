Previous work on 2D lattices involved separable coins.   \v{S}tefa\v{n}\'ak et al.\
\cite{Stefanak2010} stated that the four-state walk
on a 2D lattice admits $N = 2$ revivals but no
longer cycles within the class considered.
Later Dodangodage \cite{Dodangodage2018} constructed walks with
revivals of longer period and Jayakody \cite{Jayakody2018} showed that a non-separable coin can
be designed to produce revivals of any desired period.  In that construction the period is a free
parameter: the coin and shift are built so that the walk returns after any chosen number of steps.
Our classification covers the separable case, where the (parameterised) coin and shift are fixed 
to the standard forms on each axis and the period is not chosen but emerges from the walk itself.
We consider the two results to be complementary. Jayakody answers whether a revival of any given period
can be built.  More recently, Steinfurth et al. \cite{Steinfurth2026} demonstrated revivals experimentally using coupled optical-fibre loops.  By changing the coin over time in a way that respects a simple mirror symmetry, their scheme can produce a revival at almost any chosen period for any input state, because the coin is modulated rather than fixed.
