\par
A further general feature is the distinction between square and
non-square tori. Every square torus with $L_x = L_y$ admits a
revival: these are $2\times2$, $3\times3$, $4\times4$, $5\times5$, $6\times6$, $8\times8$, $10\times10$. Non-square tori revive
precisely when their 1D revival-period sets intersect, and the
reduced classification lists only one orientation of each such
pair. On the non-square tori, revivals are typically realised with
asymmetric reflection parameters ($\rho_x \neq \rho_y$), whereas
square tori frequently admit both symmetric and asymmetric
realisations.
