\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
We now interpret the classification of Section~\ref{sec:results}.
The reduced dataset comprises $4773$ parameter sets across $24$
tori and $30$ revival periods. Three structural features organise
the data. First, the period spectrum is sharply peaked: the most
populated periods are $N=54$ ($659$), $N=58$ ($361$), and $N=30$
($332$), while most periods carry only a handful of sets. Second,
the reflection parameters take a small set of algebraic values,
with the duality $\rho \leftrightarrow 1-\rho$ holding throughout.
Third, the phase parameters are rational multiples of $\pi$, with
denominators tied to the torus dimensions.

The duality $\rho \leftrightarrow 1-\rho$ holds without exception:
wherever $(\rho_x, \rho_y, \delta_x, \delta_y)$ admits a revival at
some period $N$, so does the set obtained by replacing any
occurrence of $\rho$ with $1-\rho$ and leaving all other
parameters fixed. This follows directly from the symmetry of the
dispersion relation under $\rho \to 1-\rho$, and it halves the
number of independent reflection values that need to be tabulated.

Square tori $L \times L$ admit revivals for every $L$ in the
classification: $2\times2$, $3\times3$, $4\times4$, $5\times5$,
$6\times6$, $8\times8$, and $10\times10$. Non-square tori revive
precisely when their $1$D revival-period sets intersect, and the
reduced classification lists only one orientation of each such
pair. Revivals on non-square tori are typically asymmetric
($\rho_x \neq \rho_y$), whereas square tori frequently admit both
symmetric and asymmetric realisations.

The five most populated periods are $N=54$ ($659$ sets), $N=58$
($361$ sets), $N=30$ ($332$ sets), $N=50$ ($275$ sets), and $N=24$
($263$ sets). These are multiples of many small integers, so
several $1$D cycles share the period and the $2$D cross-product is
large. The sparsest periods are $N=2$ ($1$), $N=4$ ($16$),
$N=28$ ($23$), $N=42$ ($25$), and $N=32$ ($44$); these admit $1$D
revivals on only a small subset of cycles. The polarisation
between dense and sparse periods is a direct reflection of the
arithmetic of the $1$D revival sets.

The spectrum splits into three regimes. The short-period regime
$N \le 12$ carries $579$ sets on a small number of symmetric
tori. The intermediate regime $14 \le N \le 36$ carries $1981$
sets, dominated by cross-products of small cycles. The
long-period regime $N \ge 38$ carries $2213$ sets, spread over
the widest range of reflection and phase parameters. The longest
period, $N = 60$, occurs only on the $5 \times 5$, $5 \times 10$,
and $10 \times 10$ tori, all inherited from the pentagonal cycles
$k = 5$ and $k = 10$.

The distribution across tori is also uneven. The tori $2\times2$
($1196$ sets), $2\times4$ ($544$ sets), $2\times3$ ($500$ sets),
$2\times6$ ($427$ sets), and $4\times4$ ($426$ sets) account for
the majority of the classification, since $k = 2$ admits $1$D
revivals at every even period and any torus involving a $2$-cycle
inherits a large family of $2$D revivals. The prime cycles $k = 5$
and $k = 10$ revive only at $N = 60$, so tori involving them are
restricted to a single long period.
Table~\ref{tab:period-summary} in the appendix lists the number of
parameter sets and the tori at each period.

The reflection parameters take a small set of algebraic values:
low-denominator rationals ($1/2$, $1/3$, $1/4$, $2/3$, $3/4$) and
quadratic surds of the form $(a \pm \sqrt{b})/c$ with
$b \in \{2, 3, 5\}$. The surds fall into three families tied to
specific cycle lengths: $k = 4$ gives the $\sqrt{2}$-family,
$k = 3$ and $k = 6$ the $\sqrt{3}$-family, and $k = 5$ and
$k = 10$ the pentagonal family $(5 \pm \sqrt{5})/10$.

The phase parameters are rational multiples of $\pi$, with
denominators dividing the revival period or twice the torus
dimension. Zero phase dominates. Non-zero phases cluster at
$2\pi/5$ and $4\pi/5$ on the pentagonal tori and at $2\pi/3$ and
$4\pi/3$ on the $3$- and $6$-cycles. The role of $\delta$ is to
select, among the family of revivals at a given
$(\rho_x, \rho_y)$, those phase combinations that satisfy the
root-of-unity condition on both axes simultaneously.
