\par
Quantum full-state revivals---the exact, periodic reconstruction
of an initial wave packet---are a surprising yet fundamental
phenomenon in discrete-time quantum walks (DTQW) on symmetric
topologies. They have practical implications for quantum memory
\cite{Chandrashekar2015} and quantum state routing
\cite{Steinfurth2026}, and suggest applications in quantum
frequency division, since the walker returns to its initial
state at integer multiples of the revival period.

Intrinsic revivals are rare. They require every eigenphase
$\theta_j$ of the walk operator $U$ to be an exact rational
multiple of $2\pi$, i.e.\ $\theta_j = 2\pi m_j / N$ for
integer $m_j$ and a common period $N$. On a general topology
the momentum spectrum is dense, and the accumulation of
incommensurate phases makes revivals impossible. The natural
question, then, is: on which topologies and for which coin
parameters can revivals occur?
